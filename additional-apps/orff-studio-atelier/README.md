## Workflows

- Collection: family browsing, search and persistent favorites.
- Instrument: touch / pointer / keyboard performance using the same isolated artwork shown in the collection; 13-note xylophone/metallophone/glockenspiel layouts and eight individually playable contrabass bars (C2–C3); C, F and G swap bars; pentatonic and individual playing-bar setup.
- Recorders: separate acoustic SATB recordings with 25 chromatic pitches each, a touch keyboard, hold/release playing, measured live note lengths, smooth sustain loops, and correct printed octave conventions. Soprano C5–C7, alto F4–F6, tenor C4–C6, bass F3–F5 (sounding). Source: VCSL, CC0; per-sample provenance is included.
- MIDI input: connect from the sidebar, grant browser permission, and optionally set a comfortable home C once. Automatic instrument octave keeps the controller in the same register: its home C plays C5/C4/C3 on soprano/alto/bass xylophones and metallophones. Pitch names are preserved, including F-based recorder ranges; inactive bars and out-of-range notes remain silent. Percussion strokes use successive white keys. Select an input and channel, or use all inputs/channels. Velocity, held pitched-note lengths and sustain-pedal releases are recorded in the selected part. Instrument changes, focus loss, disconnect and Panic release held voices. Preferences are saved locally; connecting requires an explicit action each session. This uses Web MIDI without SysEx and needs a supporting browser, such as desktop Chrome or Edge; open the studio in its own tab if embedding blocks permission. MIDI output and MIDI-file import/export are not included.
- MIDI dynamics: Light / Normal / Firm / Fixed touch curves, minimum and maximum strength (1–127), fixed strength, a last-strike Keyboard → Played meter, and Dynamic tone (0–100%). Preferences are local to the browser; default is Normal, full range, 50% tone. Each MIDI note stores its shaped velocity and optional `dynamicTone` amount. Soft notes receive a gentle per-voice high-shelf reduction and non-resonant low-pass filter, with profiles for wood, drums, metal, rattles and recorders. Pitched profiles follow the sounding register; metallophones retain their existing yarn-mallet voicing. Full-velocity strikes retain baseline tone. The source attack envelope, tuning and natural decay remain unchanged. On-screen and computer-keyboard playing, older notes and tone-off notes retain their previous behavior. Playback, reusable sections and WAV/MP3 export read each recorded note’s own settings rather than current controller preferences.
- Added percussion: tambourine hit/shake, short/long egg-shaker strokes, short/long maracas, sleigh-bell hits/shakes and soft/strong cabasa rubs. All use separate recorded acoustic performances. The contrabass voice adapts separately recorded soft marimba strikes, level-matched to the previous medium layer for a less overtone-heavy bass tone; it is not a sampled Sonor/Peripole contrabass instrument. Per-file licensing and processing provenance is included in `dist/assets/audio/recorded/ensemble-additions/`.
- Wind chimes: five separate acoustic bar-chime performances from the supplied original CC0 WAV. Select down brisk/slow or up brisk/medium/slow; each retains its native speed, stereo image and 16-second tail. These distinct gestures are explicitly selected, not randomized. Existing note-84 parts use the downward brisk take. Source hashes and processing are in `dist/assets/audio/recorded/wind-chimes/`.
- Sequencer: 1–64 measures, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 and 6/8, live overdubbing, count-in, quarter/eighth/sixteenth quantization, editable note length / pitch / velocity / position, phrase duplication, mute, solo, level and pan.
- Song Builder: reusable named sections, independent variations, drag or arrow reordering, 1–16 plays per appearance, and instrument entrances per appearance. Up to 32 reusable sections, 64 form entries, and 512 arranged measures. Recording always edits the active section; playback can follow that section or the full song.
- Notation: editable score from the shared note events; polyphonic voices, ties, rests, student part / teacher score, paginated PDF download with a matching page preview, MusicXML export. Choose the current section or the full song, with repeats written out and section cues. Clicking an arranged note edits its reusable source section.
- Sound: original Boomwhacker tubano recordings; five pitches of the VCSL frame drum with an 18-semitone span (lowest retained, evenly spaced); VCSL gong prepared to 30 seconds; recorded finger cymbals; five individual hard-mallet strikes from an acoustic composite temple-block set (CC0, Freesound public HQ preview), with native pitches, balanced levels and trimmed quiet tails. Existing temple-block parts retain their pad/staff identities. Metallophones use register-aware high-shelf and low-pass EQ for a darker yarn-mallet strike while retaining their original onset and decay. Other instruments use the previous GeneralUser GS samples, with a freshly rendered GM vibraslap. See the embedded sound credits for provenance and approximation details.
- Added percussion: acoustic agogô low/high bells, open/muted cowbell and slapstick clap, with consistent generated artwork in the gallery and player. Five native VCSL recordings (CC0) are mapped per instrument. All strokes work in playback, sections, notation and audio export. Preparation script and source hashes are retained; image prompts are in `dist/assets/instruments/bells-slapstick-provenance.json`. Projects support up to 64 parts, including the full 34-instrument collection.
- Woodblock: two separate acoustic wooden-block recordings replace the SoundFont low/high pair. Low uses VCSL; high uses Joseph Sardin's studio-recorded Meinl wood block. Both CC0, with native pitches, balanced levels and short natural decays. Existing note identities remain compatible with saved parts. Sources, hashes and processing are retained in `dist/assets/audio/recorded/woodblock/PROVENANCE.json` and `scripts/prepare-woodblock.py`.
- Exports: Web Audio offline stereo rendering, full natural tails and shared room / delay mix; PCM WAV at 44.1 kHz / 16-bit; MP3 at 192 kbps via vendored lamejs in a worker. Full-song audio and notation use the same expanded order, repeat counts and entrance gates.
- Projects: browser autosave, previous-save recovery, undo/redo, validated `.orff` JSON import/export. Earlier single-pattern files become section A without changing their notes.

## Score selection and phrase editing

In Compose → Score, Select supports an individual note, Ctrl/Command-click toggles, Shift-click time ranges, and dragging a box around noteheads. Measure-number buttons select a whole measure in the current part. Shift-click another number to extend the measure range. Delete and Clear keep the time in place and preserve notes outside the selection, including the surviving pieces of ties. Clear defaults to This part only; Whole ensemble is an explicit choice. Remove measure space is a separate, confirmed command that removes time across all parts of the active section and shifts later music earlier together. It cannot remove the section's last measure. Repeated song appearances follow the section edit. Undo and Redo restore the whole edit.

Pitched parts map by written register; unavailable pitches and incompatible percussion strokes are rejected before changing any notes. A paste must fit within the section; add measures first if necessary. Copying a full measure preserves its leading and trailing silence. Selection highlights and measure controls are excluded from printing.

### Direct note-duration correction
