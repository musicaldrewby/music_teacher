Making movies with felt

Sing with a recorded song: In Present, open Music & timing. Ordinary stories can choose Song timing from the presentation controls. Import your MP3, select Record timing, and start the song. The first scene starts at zero; press Next or Space whenever the next line begins. Pause and continue, or use Back to undo the last cue. Let the song end, or finish on the last scene to set an earlier ending. Review / edit cue times lets you adjust the starts. Finishing the recording activates its timing throughout the timeline, playback, and every MP4 export. Present opens in Follow music when a recording is available; choose Manual for student-controlled advances. An unfinished replacement take keeps the completed recording safe. Recovery saves your work on this device; Continue on the welcome screen reopens the latest story. Download story keeps a separate file with the MP3 and cues together. Export MP4 creates a full-screen 1080p video at 60 or 30 fps with optional large lyrics. Manual mode still waits for the students. Download classroom player creates a standalone HTML with embedded animations, large controls, lyrics, and optional recorded music playback. Students can open the file without the editor or an internet connection. Replacing the song or changing scene order requires new timing.

Whole-film production: Film beside Script opens one shared timeline for scenes, shots, music, voices, actions and named markers. Attach a sound, story action or pose change to a music marker; shifting a scene cut keeps linked cues at their movie times when they still fit. Import MIDI to review track entrances, tempo changes and written markers. Poses & actions mirrors complete illustrations or switches complete artwork; it never rotates a face separately from hair. Imported poses should share a matching transparent canvas. Narrator keeps a saved voice, pronunciation overrides and individually regenerated clips. Review checks voice overlaps, allotted time, long holds and facing, and renders beginning/middle/end frames. Resume Precise export reuses completed sections. Download last video again avoids rendering twice while this tab remains open.

Cinematic workflow: Movie tools → Camera now groups shot framing, movement and transitions. Select characters and choose a frame size. Continue previous camera links shots; Reaction & return keeps the scene length. Choreography gives a character or attached scarf one editable action instead of many keys. Lighting → Lighting across the movie adds a continuous sunrise across cuts. Timing → Locked movie cues keeps musical accents at their movie time. Precise export reuses completed sections automatically; cancel and export again to resume. Try File → Cinematic workflow example.

Story tools: Script in the workspace bar lists dialogue across your movie. Choose a scene and speaker, write a line, set its delivery and pause, then Save & generate voice. Update changes only that line; Shift later dialogue preserves following gaps. Camera and manually timed actions stay put. Movie tools → Camera → Conversation shots creates editable coverage for two cast members; Follow script uses the dialogue timing. Scenes → More → Continue from scene ending (or playhead) carries poses, props and framing into a new scene with fresh timing. A continued character keeps its sampled pose until you choose a pose in Acting. Collection → Interactive props includes a book, gift and door with starting states and timed open/close changes. Sound → Lower music during speech balances narration and generated dialogue; imported dialogue cues can also be marked as speech.

Director workflow: Movie tools → Storyboard opens a scene list and shot cards. Plan action and dialogue, reorder shots, then return to the canvas. Move timed cues follows shot reorders and duration changes; animation paths and manual keys remain on the scene timeline. Movie tools has Camera, Lighting, Look, Effects, Text and Timing tabs. Cinematic presets create single editable clips: select a clip to change its preset, start, duration or strength, then Update clip. Fine controls include placement, fades and camera framing. Use Lighting for overlapping lights, Camera for focus pulls, and Effects for local particles and animated ground shadows. Look can apply a color preset to one scene or every scene. Camera moves can hold their ending framing until the next shot cut; focus pulls can hold their final subject. The offstage workspace keeps the cinematic result inside the video frame. Camera tracking follows a moving piece, while Look → Focus keeps selected depth layers sharp. Timing offers markers, a manual tempo grid and snapping. Sound cues and narration can lead into a scene or continue across its cut. When enabled, they no longer extend that scene. In Acting → Speech from audio, select imported narration or a sound cue, analyze it and set this character’s speaking range. Volume drives the mouth; there is no speech recognition. Export offers whole movie, current scene or a marked range, 1080p or a smaller 540p draft, a looping range review, and movie checks. Storyboard’s Director Plan menu imports or exports editable JSON for scripts and automation.

Movie tools: Open Movie tools above the board for a sequence of shots within one scene, lighting, weather, opening titles, rolling credits, and a dialogue list. Use the small playhead to scrub and Play scene to preview. Add a shot, choose Still or Smooth move, then use Edit start / Edit end and the camera controls. Shot length adjusts the scene length; narration or later cues can extend it. Shots keep the same character animation running through each cut. Camera-key scenes remain supported; clear their camera motion before using a shot sequence. Scene entrances include fade, dissolve, iris, and felt curtains. File → Open cinematic example demonstrates these tools.

Cast, depth, and accessories: Collection → Pieces → Cast offers four felt characters with seven expressions, seven poses, blinking and a talking mouth. Select one to open Acting. Add a cue for a timed change; its default expression returns at the end. Talking is an adjustable visual loop, not automatic lip-sync. To hold an object, select the accessory, open Placement & accessories, choose its character and hand, and click Attach. Drag it to adjust its held position. Let go at freezes it in place at that time. Detach here preserves the current position. Attached objects follow their character instead of their own motion blocks. Foreground frames are separate transparent pieces. Placement chooses background, middle or foreground depth; near layers move more during camera pans.

Character speech: Select a cast member and open Talk. Natural offers six distinct local English voices, with Normal, Cheerful, Excited, Calm, Worried and Sleepy delivery. Hear voice previews it; Add line stores speech and mouth timing. First use downloads the voice pack. Existing saved voices keep their sound. To replace older robot speech, choose Natural, then Update line or Fine-tune voice → Use this voice for all character lines. The latter rebuilds this character’s lines in this scene and shifts later dialogue; review acting and camera timing afterward. Cancel keeps the story unchanged; Undo restores a completed replacement. Natural delivery changes pacing and phrasing, rather than offering full emotional voice acting.

Dialogue and sound cues: Select a bubble to set a start time, duration and word-by-word reveal. Movie tools → Text lists every line. Sound includes a cue timeline: add a synthesized effect or import a recording, drag its bar to move it, and set fades, volume, loop and audio offset. These settings are included in playback, exported video, downloaded stories and browser recovery. Collection’s New · Movie collection filter and six story-pack filters find the new artwork. Full artwork loads only when used.

Selection toolbar: Click a piece to get Duplicate, Delete and More right beside it. Shift-click or drag across empty space to select several pieces; Group then appears. Select a group for Ungroup. More offers lock, layer order and alignment. Locked pieces are kept when you duplicate or delete a mixed selection; the button shows how many pieces it affects. Undo in the top toolbar restores a deletion. These controls work in Build and Animate and stay out of exported images and videos.

Build a scene: choose a backdrop, add story pieces, and drag them into place. Use corner handles to resize, the top handle to rotate, or the inspector for exact values. You can still move unlocked pieces while presenting.

Make your own: Draw felt makes a closed shape. Polygon places one corner per click; press Enter or Finish shape to close it. Felt pen makes a thick freehand line. Escape cancels a drawing. Colors and stitches also apply to new shapes.

Comic dialogue: Click + Comic text beside Draw in the toolbar, or Collection → Comic text, for speech, thought, shout, whisper, and caption styles. Choose a style, then type in the Comic text properties section or double-click a bubble to edit. Set its tail, colors, and font; Fit text keeps words inside. Box dimensions reflow the words, while corner handles scale the entire bubble. Group bubbles with characters to move or animate them together. Dialogue is included in saved stories, PNGs, and videos.

Combine: hold Shift while clicking, use Multi-select, or drag across empty board space. Group keeps colors; Boolean operations make one felt silhouette in the current color. Subtract uses the lowest layer as the base. Difference removes overlaps. Ungroup recovers the operands, including after a Boolean.

Tell your story: add or copy scenes along the bottom. Present hides the editing panels; use arrow keys to turn pages. Captions appear above the presentation controls.

Share a standalone story: In Export or Present, choose Standalone classroom story. Download one HTML file and send it to another teacher. Open the file in Chrome or Edge, then use Space or Next to advance each cue. Audio & timing lets the receiving teacher add a song, tap through to record timing, and review cue times. Auto follows the song; Manual waits for each press. Save a copy keeps the audio and timing in a new HTML file. No account or studio access is needed.

Keep your work: Edits are saved automatically as recovery copies in this browser on this device. File → Recover recent story opens one. Download story creates an editable JSON file with imported images, music, narration and My pieces. Keep that file as a lasting backup or open it on another device. Recovery is not cloud storage; clearing site data removes it.

Music notation: Find symbols under Pieces → Music notes & rests, and staves under Backdrops → Music staves. Notes start at the staff’s spacing. Drag them onto a line or space; resize and recolor as needed. Whole rests hang from a line; half rests sit on one. Guide-line versions make that difference visible away from a staff. Blank staff has five lines and no clef.

Music, narration, and MP4: Open Sound to import music across scenes and narration for each scene. Open Export for a 16:9, 1920 × 1080 MP4 at 60 fps or 30 fps. Precise mode encodes every frame; Compatibility captures in real time. Availability depends on your browser. Preview selected range checks your sound and animation before export.

Animation blocks: Open Animate and select a piece or group. Choose + Animation block for 70 presets across Entrance, Idle, Emphasis, Path and Exit. Each preset stays one block. Click a block to edit its timing, strength, easing and loops; drag it to move it or drag either end to resize it. Replace preset keeps its start and duration. Preview block plays that motion on its own.

Idle and sequence: Idle blocks can loop until the next exit or scene end. Their Duration field becomes Cycle length. Resizing a looping idle block makes it a fixed-length motion. Entrance, Idle and Exit have one block each per piece; adding another replaces that lane. Paths and emphasis may have several blocks, with no overlap within the same lane. Completed paths hold their endpoint so later paths continue from there.

Move your cutout: Pieces with blocks keep a separate resting pose. Dragging or resizing changes that resting pose without adding keys. Check Edit resting pose to see the original placement while editing; playback and export still use the animation. Copy animation and Paste animation transfer motion to another piece at its own location.

Paths: Select a piece, open Animate, then click Draw path. Drag from its Start marker along the route you want; release to finish. To reshape a curve, choose Simple curve and drag Start, Bend or End. Custom curve presets open these handles automatically. Draw route replaces a route when you finish a new stroke; Escape cancels a stroke. The timeline minimizes while you work. Click Done, then Play scene. Each route remains one block. Undo restores the previous route. The timeline stays below the board while the selected block’s settings appear in the side panel. Minimize and Expand adjust the timeline.

Existing keyframes: Old stories keep their keys under Advanced. Clear all animation removes all blocks and keys from the selected unlocked pieces in one action. Clear a type removes only that lane. Convert blocks to keyframes is optional and samples the result into editable keys; long scenes use fewer samples per second. Undo returns to blocks. Group pieces before adding animation.

Playback and saving: The timeline previews at 60 fps. Play scene includes narration and background music. Download story includes blocks and keys. PNG exports the playhead pose; MP4 evaluates motion at every output frame at 30 or 60 fps. Use this version or newer to open stories with animation blocks.

Offstage: Click Offstage to see a larger editing area around the gold video frame. Drag a piece outside that frame to park it offscreen. It remains selectable there, and Draw path works across the frame. A piece dragged completely out of the shot is revealed automatically when you release it. Fit all shows pieces and their routes. Find selected centers the editing view without moving a piece; the offscreen-piece list and Layers can recover lost pieces. Wheel zooms the workspace; Space-drag, middle-drag or Pan view pans it. Shot view returns to the camera shot. These editor controls do not change your camera, story, PNG or MP4. Hidden or transparent selected pieces have a faint editing preview only.

Workspaces: Build arranges pieces, Animate opens a compact timeline with settings beside the board, Sound adds music and narration, and Export creates video or images. Collection and Properties buttons reopen hidden panels. Focus hides both side panels. Workspace zoom only changes your editing view; Camera zoom changes the recorded shot. Quick tour walks through your first story.

Conversation acting: Select Maya, Leo, Asha, or Jun, then click Acting beside the selection. Conversation performance follows saved Talk dialogue with gestures and listening. Select a clip to edit or delete it. Movie tools → Camera → Conversation shots → Dialogue aware suggests shots from the saved voices; review the plan, then apply. File → Conversation acting example opens a complete voiced scene.

Shortcuts: V select, D draw, P polygon, Ctrl/⌘ A select all, Ctrl/⌘ D duplicate, Ctrl/⌘ G group, Ctrl/⌘ Z undo, arrow keys nudge, Delete remove. Locked pieces cannot be moved or edited until unlocked.
