# Soundroom

Browser SoundFont workstation with eight MIDI tracks and the included GeneralUser GS 1.471 bank. Static hosting serves `dist/`; sound generation, recordings and project files stay in the visitor's browser.

## Editing and building

- `src/index.html`: page, help and licenses.
- `src/app.js` / `src/lcd-browser.css` / `src/ui.css`: LCD pages for sounds, performances, shaping and combined effects; common workspace frame, Project menu, touch keyboard, effects and audio startup.
- `src/workspace-layout.js` / `src/workspace-layout.css`: viewport frame, persistent navigation/transport, contextual nonmodal drawers, Settings tabs and responsive workspace organization.
- `src/studio.js`: transport, recording, piano-roll editor, mixer and project-file controls.
- `src/workstation.js` / `src/workstation.css`: PLAY, PATTERN, SONG and MIXER page controls, eight named scenes, drum steps, expression lanes, recovery and export UI.
- `src/composition-model.js` / `src/composition-ui.js`: clock-independent step entry, validated performance presets and visual song timeline.
- `src/arrangement.js`: pattern range operations, controller chasing, scene order and repeats.
- `src/recovery.js`: browser-local autosave and previous-session checkpoint.
- `src/export-formats.js`: format-1 MIDI, PCM WAV headers and uncompressed ZIP packaging.
- `src/audio-export.js` / `src/export-worker.js`: background SoundFont rendering for stereo mix and aligned individual tracks.
- `src/sequencer-model.js`: shared eight-part performance and eight-pattern song schema, legacy migration, recording, reversible quantization, score compilation and audio timeline.
- `src/sequencer-worklet.js`: audio-thread scheduler and metronome, extending the pinned SpessaSynth processor.
- `src/metronome.js`: four dry click voices, shared by sample-timed count-ins, transport playback and cancellable one-bar previews.
- `src/audio-clock.js`: maps the playhead and externally monitored input to the audible output clock, with a latency fallback. Internally monitored recording uses worklet rendering timestamps instead.
- `src/performance-engine.js`: shared-note ownership and MIDI parsing.
- `src/synth-effects.js`: internal GS effect messages and delay-time conversions.
- `src/soundfonts.json`: bank metadata, local asset paths, hashes and preset catalogs.
- `src/hardware.css`, `src/performance.css`, `src/studio.css`: hardware surface, green/purple LCDs, touch controls and responsive layout.
- `src/make_lcd_font.py`: original dense 5×7 pixel font; generates `dist/assets/soundroom-lcd.woff` with fontTools.

`npm run build` bundles the app, copies HTML/CSS and builds the custom worklet into `dist`. It uses the local `spessasynth-processor-4.3.14.js` asset as its pinned base. `npm test` exercises the project model, real page handlers with a lightweight Node DOM stub, and actual sample/effect rendering. The shipped worklet is also rendered in a Node VM to check all eight instruments, pan, volume and cancellation. Synchronization regressions cover render-clock gaps, delayed Play commands, three successive MIDI overdubs with output latency and callback delay, deferred start acknowledgments, and eight-track timing across repeated loops and live score updates at 44.1 and 48 kHz. Mode regressions verify replacement isolation and undo; loop-note tests cover quantization, full duration, and restoring original timing. These tests do not verify browser layout or device behavior.

GeneralUser GS is split into four binary assets and reassembled byte for byte. Its SHA-256 is `f45b6b4a68b6bf3d792fcbb6d7de24dc701a0f89c5900a21ef3aaece993b839a`. Engine and bank licenses are in Help & credits. Only the included GeneralUser bank is available. Custom SoundFont upload and bank switching have been removed.

## Workstation behavior

- The four workspaces share a viewport frame: navigation and Project actions above, a scrolling workspace in the center, and a persistent transport below. PLAY keeps the fixed-height LCD above its compact track strip and keyboard; wide screens place the eight scene triggers beside the LCD, while smaller screens stack them. PATTERN has a compact scene row, a single track selector, and mutually exclusive Track overview / Piano roll / Drum steps editors. Its keyboard is optional. SONG focuses on the timeline and one selected-entry editor; MIXER focuses on eight strips and master volume. Green/Purple, Compact and Full screen remain global controls.

- Save song and Undo/Redo stay in the project bar. The native Project disclosure groups Open song, New song, Restore previous session and an Export music shortcut. Export opens a shared drawer and focuses its heading without switching workspace or replacing project data. The menu closes after choosing an action, switching workspaces, clicking outside or pressing Escape. While it is open, computer-note and scene shortcuts are suppressed; Escape still stops sound. Play, Record, Stop and Save have stronger visual emphasis than reset, copy and clear actions. All new menu controls retain at least 44 px touch targets.

- The large LCD is the sound browser. Tap its instrument name or ALL / GM / VARIATIONS / DRUMS to open instrument pages. Each page has eight sounds in two columns (four sounds in one column below 520 px). Search reveals name/program/bank search and instrument-family filtering. Page controls clamp after filtering. Tapping a sound loads it into the selected shared performance part; the browser stays open for keyboard/demo auditioning. Done restores the patch home display. The part number/name remains in the LCD header, the selected sound is highlighted, and the loaded sound is listed above the choices. The former sidebar is consolidated into this display, retaining both LCD themes and touch-sized controls.

- PLAY, PATTERN, SONG and MIXER views share one eight-part performance and exactly eight independent scene patterns. Each scene has its own name, notes, controllers, punch range and length of 1–256 measures. Instruments, track names, shaping and mixer settings are shared across all scenes. Defaults: four measures per pattern, 4/4, 100 BPM, sixteenth-note visual grid, editing snap on and sixteenth-note recording quantization. Record settings groups Record to grid, Overdub/Record over, count-in, punch, meter and loop; the transport summarizes the armed track, record mode and grid. Track 8 starts with a drum kit; any track can use any available patch.
- Real-time recording captures note timing, velocity, sustain, modulation, expression, pitch bend and channel pressure from the selected MIDI input. The touch and computer keyboards also record. One armed track records at a time, while the others play. Notes within the last 150 ms before the first downbeat are captured at beat one, preserving played duration and velocity. This also works with quantization Off. Earlier count-in notes are ignored; matching near-start controller events are anchored at beat one.
- Visual grid, editing snap and recording quantization are independent. Opening a saved project preserves its recording-quantization choice, including Off. Grids range from whole notes to sixty-fourths, with quarter/eighth/sixteenth triplets. Post-recording quantization has strength, swing and optional note-length adjustment. Restore timing recovers the original performance until a manual timing edit establishes a new baseline.
- The Record settings panel offers Overdub (keep existing notes and add) or Record over (replace the armed track with a fresh take). Record over clears only that track in the selected scene once when Record is pressed; subsequent loop passes add to the new take. Undo restores the earlier take. A played note is stored whole even when it spans a loop boundary; display/playback segments are derived afterward. Quantization therefore moves the whole note without creating duplicate downbeat attacks. Original timing and full duration survive save/load and Restore timing. With loop off, recording ends at the pattern boundary and clips held notes there.
- The piano roll supports drawing, moving, resizing, erasing, group selection, note properties, velocity, transposition, duplication and copying measure ranges. Use Scroll mode for touch panning. Note editing is disabled during playback. Track editing and takes have undo/redo history.
- The eight scene buttons select independent patterns with custom names (48 characters). While stopped, selection is immediate. Number keys 1–8 (top row or numeric keypad) use the same controls; shortcuts ignore held-key repeats, modifiers, text/number/select fields, contenteditable regions and Help. During pattern playback, scene changes always wait for beat 1 of the next measure, including requests made on the current downbeat. The latest choice replaces the pending choice, with a flashing indicator. Scene selection has no cancel control. Older saved switch preferences normalize to this measure-only behavior. The audio worklet releases outgoing sequenced notes and starts the new pattern at the exact sample boundary, retaining the shared instruments, live voices and effects engine. Scene changes are locked during recording. The interface waits for the audible output clock before updating the selected pattern.
- SONG arranges references to scenes, with reordering and 1–64 repeats per entry. Editing a scene updates every song occurrence. The order supports up to 256 entries and 16,384 total measures; generated playback/export scores have an event limit. Song playback runs once and its scene lights follow the arrangement. Pattern recording/editing remains separate from this derived song timeline. Copy a pattern to another scene to create a variation; destination naming remains intact. Clear pattern removes only its musical events. Copy/clear and arrangement edits have Undo.
- PATTERN measure copy, duplicate, move and erase support the selected track or all eight in the current scene. Notes crossing boundaries are clipped/copied; controller state is chased at scene starts and restored at punched/erased boundaries.
- Punch-in/out records only its selected measures; Record over clears only that passage once. Early punch entries use the same 150 ms grace, quantization remains optional, held notes clip at punch-out, and sustain/bend/controller states restore after the range.
- PATTERN includes a named drum-step grid with 44 px steps, per-hit velocity, measure navigation and grid resolution. Recorded off-grid positions remain unchanged. Velocity bars and editable sustain, modulation, pitch bend, expression and channel-pressure lanes feed the existing playback engine. Select/scroll and numeric point editing support touch use.
- Exports include MIDI format 1 (960 PPQ, conductor tempo/meter/markers and eight instrument tracks), a stereo 44.1 kHz 16-bit PCM WAV, and eight aligned WAVs in a ZIP. A dedicated worker uses the same pinned sample engine with each track’s patch, tuning, envelope, mix and effects. WAV respects mute/solo/master and omits the metronome. Choose 2/8/15/30 seconds of tail; clipping is reported instead of silently normalizing. Estimated output over 180 MiB is rejected before rendering to bound browser memory; Cancel terminates the worker. MIDI carries events and patch/controller setup but does not embed SoundFont samples or global audio effects.
- The mixer provides per-track volume, pan, mute, solo, input monitoring, reverb send and delay send. Global reverb and delay settings live on their LCD pages in PLAY, with shortcuts from MIXER. Reverb offers room/hall/plate choices, decay, damping and pre-delay; delay offers time, feedback, damping and stereo width. Both master effect enable/amount controls and each track's send affect the result. Default sends are audible when the corresponding effect is enabled.
- Live input uses synth channels 0–7; sequenced notes use channels 8–15, so a live note-off cannot cut off the same sequenced pitch. A timeline inside the AudioWorklet schedules playback on the audio clock; it does not rely on main-thread intervals. Play/Record starts are established on the audio thread and acknowledged to the recorder, with per-run IDs to reject old messages. The worklet subdivides each audio block at note/controller event frames, applying events to the sample engine within one sample of their scheduled time. The sample engine is synchronized to the device clock every rendering block so a loading/rendering gap cannot produce a permanent playback offset. Monitored input is sent to the worklet as one packet containing live MIDI messages and recording metadata. The worklet applies those messages at the beginning of an audio block and returns that exact rendering timestamp. Recorder placement therefore matches the live sound relative to the metronome; it does not subtract output latency from the monitored performance. Notes, releases, sustain, bend and expression use this same path, including computer/touch input. The playhead and externally monitored input still use `getOutputTimestamp()` with reported-latency fallback. Original input timestamps remain useful in that external-monitor path, but cannot represent the sound heard through the app after monitoring delay. Recording streams only new note/controller events to the worklet, preserving the scheduling cursor and loop-boundary note-offs; it no longer recompiles/clones the entire score on each MIDI event. New captures enter playback on the next pass, preventing an immediate second attack when a short note quantizes ahead of the playhead. Loop-spanning tails wait until after the first full playback onset. New piano-roll notes are painted incrementally outside the input callback; the full editor and track previews refresh when the take finishes. Startup prepares the editor before posting the audio start command. Stop drains final captured inputs before acknowledging completion; Save, editing and the next take wait for that acknowledgment. Per-run IDs reject stale captures. A five-second audio-failure fallback retains pending notes with an explicit estimated-timing status instead of leaving the interface locked. Stop clears queued sequence events. Instrument releases and effect tails may continue briefly.
- Save song downloads a version-2 `.soundroom.json` file containing the shared performance once, all eight scene patterns with names/lengths/original timing, scene order/repeats, patches, mixer settings, shaping and global effects. Open validates files before replacing the song. Version-1 projects import their complete source timeline into Scene 1 and, when it fits, their arranged timeline into Scene 2. Remaining scene slots receive named legacy sections. The full original project is archived inside the song and can be downloaded from SONG, preserving sections beyond the eight available slots. SoundFont sample data is separate; projects reopen with the included GeneralUser bank. Projects using a different bank are rejected without replacing the current song. The full project, selected bank metadata and global effects automatically recover from localStorage on the same device. New/Open retain a previous-session checkpoint. Storage failures display a Save song fallback. Autosave serialization waits until transport stops so it cannot block live MIDI delivery during a take. Autosave is device-local, not cloud sync.
- Built-in samples are cached after first download; transfers use copies so the cached original remains available for exports. Download failures can be retried. Legacy recovery data for other banks is preserved with an explanatory banner.
- Per-track shaping includes octave, fine tuning, attack, release and brightness. CC73/72/74 use 64 as neutral; response depends on the SoundFont. Incoming bank/program changes are ignored. Pitch bend range is ±2 semitones. Internal GS messages are never sent to connected hardware.
- The LCD measures its pages and keeps a consistent height across menu changes, bounded to 235–330 px by viewport height in Standard and 200–280 px in Compact. Its page navigation stays visible while content scrolls. Compact also shortens the unexpanded keyboard. Compact is remembered on this device and remains independent of Expand keyboard. Toggling it or changing workspaces does not stop held notes or playback. Four fixed browser rows reserve room for two-line patch names, even on a short or empty results page. Both layouts retain at least 44 px control targets. Green keeps its existing color with stronger illumination. Purple is more saturated with a brighter glow. Theme choice persists locally. Split, layer, arpeggiator and latch remain removed.
- Metronome settings are available in Settings and from the transport’s Click shortcut. Bright click, Wood block, Rim tap and Digital beep use distinct generated waveforms with stronger output than the former quiet click; new projects default to 65%. Sound, volume and beat-one accent can change during playback without replacing the score or resetting its origin. Preview one bar uses the same audio-thread voices and the current tempo/meter while stopped; a second press, Stop, Escape or starting the transport cancels it. Previews never send MIDI or enter recorded tracks. Count-ins always click, even with the playback metronome Off; 0% volume silences them. Master volume also affects the click. Song saves and autosave include tone and accent; older files retain their stored volume and receive default tone/accent values. Exports omit both playback clicks and previews.
- Web MIDI is requested only after Connect MIDI, without SysEx permission, and requires browser support. Touch controls, responsive layouts, expanded performance view and feature-detected fullscreen remain available. Search/help text and inputs remain selectable. Blur or hidden-page state stops playback and clears held notes and sustain.

## MIDI timing adjustment

Settings → MIDI input provides a device-local recording adjustment from −250 to +250 ms, default 0. Negative moves captured MIDI earlier; positive moves it later. The correction is applied once and equally to note-on, note-off and expression timestamps before quantization. With Soundroom monitoring enabled it adjusts the acknowledged rendering time; with external monitoring it adjusts the physical input timestamp mapped to audible output. It never alters live monitoring, computer/touch keys, playback speed or the transport clock. Each selected input (or All inputs) has its own stored value. The new timing path uses a new adjustment preference key, starting at 0 rather than carrying over compensation for the former clock mismatch. It is locked during a take. Browser-reported output latency and the last MIDI delivery delay are displayed separately; these are diagnostics, not a measurement of the controller's complete physical latency. Start testing at 0 with quantization Off and wired audio, then adjust only a consistent remaining offset.

Timing regressions additionally cover live-release ordering, bounded event messages under MIDI controller traffic, deferred incremental editor paint, stop-time autosave, correction before quantization with unchanged note duration/controller timing, persistence/reset, no same-pass quantization echo, loop-spanning first-playback ordering, and streamed worklet timing at 44.1/48 kHz. Physical controller, browser and audio-device timing still require hands-on verification.

The v21 regression specifically models a live note heard on the click while the original MIDI keypress timestamp is earlier by output/dispatch latency. Three monitored overdubs retain the heard timing with differing output latencies and delayed audio receipts; quantization follows that heard onset rather than the older keypress position. Real-engine worklet tests at 44.1/48 kHz verify that input acknowledgments share the live MIDI rendering frame and that replay retains the onset/duration within sample rounding. Final note releases survive Stop, even when Start/input acknowledgments reach the main thread late. Physical controller and browser verification remain necessary.

## VR Hold mode and grip sustain (v30)

The VR sound panel now offers Strike and Hold buttons. Strike retains swept, velocity-sensitive hits and its adjustable 80–2000 ms gate. Hold begins even with gentle downward contact, uses the keyboard velocity setting, stays on while the mallet rests at the bar, and releases on lift or leaving the bar. Lost tracking, a frame discontinuity, mode changes, Stop/panic, recenter and leaving VR release contact notes; restored tracking requires a fresh lift. The bars continue to light while held. Piano/percussion samples still decay naturally.

Controller removal/tracking loss releases that controller’s contribution; recenter, input reset and exit clear both. Existing keyboard/MIDI sustain sources remain independent. The LCD shows the combined sustain state, and the footer shows the grip pedal status. ## Compose studio (v6)

COMPOSE is a separate continuous-score workspace alongside the original eight-part scene workstation. It uses up to 32 independent sampled instrument parts on audio channels 16–47; ordinary recording, patterns, song orders, saved v1/v2 files and VR remain available. The included Beethoven example retains all twelve original parts (8,295 notes) from Mutopia public-domain piece 941. Its MIDI source is credited in the project.

- Import Standard MIDI format 0/1, PPQ timing, including running status, velocities, note lengths, channel instruments/program changes, GM percussion, controllers 0–119, sustain, expression, modulation, pitch bend, channel pressure, tempo, meters and markers. Format 2, SMPTE, more than 32 parts, malformed data and resource limits fail before replacing a composition. SysEx and polyphonic aftertouch are omitted with a visible notice.
- All score positions are zero-based quarter-note beats in JSON/tools, displayed from beat 1 in the UI. Linear BPM ramps use exact logarithmic integration shared by live playback and WAV rendering. MIDI export samples ramps every quarter beat and uses separate MIDI ports for independent parts. A MIDI receiver must honor port metadata for multitimbral playback.
- Markers, precise seek, practice speed, part mute/solo, instrument/mixer changes, note entry/editing and expression ramps are editable. Sustained notes remain whole across markers; seeking restores held notes and prior expression state. Importing a scene song copies its existing rendered arrangement into Compose.
- Portable format: `soundroom-composition`, version 1. `soundroom_composition_schema` returns a valid template, patch catalog and limits. Limits: 32 parts, 200,000 playback events, 65,536 beats, four hours playback. WAV rendering is limited to approximately 38 minutes at 44.1 kHz to bound memory.
- IndexedDB song collection saves up to ten versions per song on this browser/device. This is not cloud synchronization. Download/open project moves work between devices. JSON, MIDI and WAV exports include the entire composition, with clipping reporting and a four-second WAV tail.
- Optional WebMCP tools register via `document.modelContext`. Read/get/schema, atomic composition replacement/editing, transport, save version, and export use the same validation and controls as the UI. Registration is feature-detected; unsupported browsers retain all ordinary controls.

Validation: `npm test` includes a Node DOM handler harness (not browser layout), malformed import and atomic edit checks, original MIDI round-trip, ramp integration/seek, local revision retention, real 32-part DSP rendering and the shipped AudioWorklet. No live browser/WebMCP verification is claimed when the supported browser QA plugin is unavailable.
