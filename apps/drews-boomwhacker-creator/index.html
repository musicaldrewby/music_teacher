User manual

The complete Teacher Edition guide: 32 pages of step-by-step instructions, instrument references, and troubleshooting.

Open manual (PDF) ↗Download PDF

Open this site in desktop Chrome or Edge. Load an arrangement with Open project, or start composing below. Save project downloads your arrangement, settings, and backing audio. Use Perform for your classroom display.

  1. Welcome. Start creating opens a blank arrangement; Open project loads your saved work; Explore demo opens Rainbow Groove. Welcome in the top toolbar brings this screen back. Continue editing returns to your current song. Turn off Show at startup to go straight to the editor next time in this browser.
  2. Find your settings. Use Song for title and tempo, Instruments for tubes and percussion, Sound for backing audio and guide notes, and Stage for 3D or 2D play view, backgrounds, and rehearsal controls. Section time signature, measures, and grid snap are beside the note editor.
  3. Build with sections. Select a section in the editor or click its song card. Create Intro, A, B, Bridge, or Coda patterns. Add them to the song, change repeat counts, and drag cards or use the arrow buttons to reorder. Editing A updates every A. Click Copy section on any song card to insert an independent copy immediately after it, with the same repeat count. Copy section above the editor copies the selected pattern once into the song. Copies open for editing automatically; use Rename for a new name or Undo to remove a copy. “Make page a section” copies the visible measures into a new pattern. Use Preview section to rehearse one pattern and Play song for the full form.
  4. Change meter between sections. Each section can use 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, or 6/8. Its grid, metronome, lyrics, and count-in follow that meter. Song cards show the signature, and playback flows directly between sections. For 6/8, Keep main pulse makes the two dotted-quarter beats follow Song tempo; Keep note values preserves quarter-note speed. Meter changes preserve existing note positions and re-bar populated sections. Copy a section to create a variation. Audio alignment markers may need review after changing the song structure.
  5. Clear the top of the stage. Click Hide song info above the stage, including in Perform mode, or uncheck Show song info in Stage settings. This hides the title, Music Lab name, tempo, section, and measure information. Lyrics, note labels, the count-in, and instrument setup screens stay available. You can switch it while music plays; Save project remembers the choice.
  6. Make room on a laptop. Hide settings expands the play area and editor. Show settings brings the controls back; Stage settings opens the Stage tab directly. Smaller windows start with settings tucked away. Long dialogs scroll, and the note grid, song order, and bar racks can scroll independently.
  7. Choose your play view. In Stage settings, choose 3D Stage for the perspective runway or 2D Play Field for straight falling-note lanes with no scenery. Both work in classroom performance. Use Perform or Full screen to fill the screen. Your instrument intro and lyrics remain available; your 3D background is remembered when you switch back.
  8. Choose an ending. In Stage → Ending message, choose the default, write your own message, or select No message. This setting saves with your project.
  9. Press Play to try Rainbow Groove. Give each group the tube matching its lane. Strike when a block reaches the line.
  10. Create a pattern. Click the grid to place strikes. Stacked blocks play together. Click a block to hear it and fill in Precise note entry; drag it to move. Use Erase, right-click, or Remove strike to delete silently. Preview notes is on by default; switch it off in either note editor to work silently. Your browser remembers this choice. Previews use the selected sound and mixer level, including bass substitutions and metallophone dampers. Changing pitch previews the new note; resizing or moving only in time does not replay it. Repeat page copies up to four measures after the visible page.
  11. Make a roll. Drag the small grip on a note’s right edge to extend it. A zigzag marks repeated playing for that entire length, in the editor and on the stage. Drag the body to move it; a roll stops at its section boundary. Use Precise note entry to choose Roll, its exact length, and relaxed, fast, or very fast roll speed, then Add / update. In 6/8, lengths use eighth-note subdivisions. Choose Single strike and Add / update to remove a roll at that position. Rolls use quick, uneven taps with subtle touch and tone differences; their speed is independent of the song tempo. Copying sections or repeating pages includes rolls.
  12. Add percussion. Enable any of the fifteen optional instrument tracks. Click Hear to audition a sound. Place strikes in its labeled editor row just like tube notes. Instrument pictures identify moving cues and strike pads, including when note names are hidden. Percussion tracks repeat and copy with their sections. Disable Play percussion sounds for students to supply those sounds themselves. Save project retains enabled tracks and hidden strikes.
  13. Arrange your percussion. Drag a percussion name on the grid up or down, or use the drag handles and arrow buttons in Instruments. The first percussion row becomes the leftmost percussion lane in performance. Your order also appears on the opening instrument screen. Reordering keeps each instrument’s notes and rolls together. Hidden instruments remember their position. Save project stores the order, and Undo / Redo restores order changes. Press Escape while dragging to cancel.
  14. Add your music. Load a local audio file, set its tempo, then enter the time of its first downbeat. Match the arrangement’s length to your song. BPM sets the note grid; it does not change the speed of imported audio. For recordings that change tempo, use Align to audio to mark measure starts or main beats.
  15. Import MIDI. Choose the tracks containing the student parts. MIDI note starts become strikes in the selected or a new section. Tempo changes repeat with the section, scaled to the song tempo. The default range is C4–C5. Under Available tubes, Add lower octave extends the range down to C3, using bass tubes or Octavator caps. Add higher octave extends it up to C6. Enable both for three octaves, C3–C6. Extended ranges use octave-number labels, with LOWER and HIGHER markings and a glowing red line between registers. C5 appears once, in the higher group when that octave is enabled. MIDI notes within your enabled range retain their pitch; notes outside it can be octave-folded or omitted. Turning an added octave off hides and mutes its extra parts while keeping their notes saved.
  16. Prepare your instruments. Stage → Presentation offers One group at a time or All together. Group mode introduces low notes, regular Boomwhackers, high sample pitches, unpitched percussion, and each barred instrument separately. Only used instruments appear; unpitched parts split into pages of up to six. Instruments fly in, hold, and fade away in place. Choose 3, 5, 8, or 12 seconds after arrival; bar diagrams get at least 6 seconds. Preview introduction has pause and Previous/Next controls. Choose your low-note equipment in Instruments. Octavator labels identify the tube to hold and the lower pitch it plays. High notes use pitch cards to match on your chosen instrument. New songs start in group mode; older saves retain All together until changed.
  17. Intro backgrounds. Use Stage → Intro background or Browse backgrounds… to choose from 17 backgrounds, including Theater Velvet, Sunlit Adobe, Rainy Window, and Leaf Shadows. Underwater Light has moving caustics. For the three animated backgrounds, choose Still, Slow, Gentle, or Lively and adjust the light/effect intensity. Reduced-motion settings keep the background still. Instrument arrivals and soft drop shadows keep their usual appearance. Lettering adapts to the background. The same colors continue across groups, then fade to the empty stage for two seconds before the count-in. Older background choices load using their closest new color blend.
  18. Add Tubanos. In Instruments → Percussion tracks, enable Tubano · Low, Medium, or High. Each has its own recording, illustrated track, and Mixer channel. Use Hear to preview it, add strikes in its grid row, or extend a note into a roll. Their default order is low to high; drag to change it. Older Tubano parts open as Medium, keeping their notes and mixer settings. These are separate from the three hand drums.
  19. Add hand drums. In Instruments → Percussion tracks, turn on Hand drum · Low, Medium, or High. Each has its own sound and illustrated track. Use Hear to preview it, click its grid row to add strikes, or drag a note’s right edge for a roll. Only instruments used in the song appear on the opening screen.
  20. Add jingle bells. In Instruments → Percussion tracks, enable Jingle bells for a handheld circular bell ring. Use Hear to preview its shake, add individual notes for short shakes, or extend a note into a roll for continuous shaking. Its graphic appears in performance and on the opening screen when used in the song.
  21. Add maracas or güiro. In Instruments → Percussion tracks, enable Maracas for a shake or Güiro for a scrape. Click Hear to preview their recorded WAV sounds. Add strikes or extend a note into a roll for repeated shaking or scraping. Both have instrument pictures, draggable track order, and their own mixer channels.
  22. Add vibraslap. In Instruments → Percussion tracks, enable Vibraslap. Click Hear to preview its recording, then add strikes in its editor row. Its wooden ball, bent metal handle, and resonator box identify its notes and its place on the opening instrument screen. Drag a note longer for repeated taps.
  23. Recorded percussion. Tubano · Low, Medium, and High, Triangle, Wood block, Cowbell, Gathering drum, Tambourine, Jingle bells, Vibraslap, Maracas, and Güiro use embedded recordings by default. The Jingle bells part uses the embedded Sleigh Bell recording and keeps its handheld bell-ring picture. In Instruments, Percussion sound lets you choose Recorded samples or Synthesized guide. Use Hear to preview each sound. Recordings play at their original pitch and speed in rehearsal and rolls. The three hand drums keep their synthesized sounds. The recordings are included in this HTML and work offline.
  24. Recorded Boomwhackers. The 22 embedded Boomwhacker recordings cover the natural notes from C3 through C6. In Sound, choose Recorded Boomwhackers or Synthesized guide, and use Hear C4 to preview. The recorded option uses D, E, G, A, and B one semitone lower for each octave’s C♯, E♭, F♯, A♭, and B♭. The same samples and tuning play in rehearsal and rolls. Recordings are built into this offline HTML; saved projects keep your sound choice and octave settings.
  25. Glockenspiels. In Instruments → Mallet instruments → Glockenspiels, enable Alto glockenspiel (C5–A6), Soprano glockenspiel (C6–A7), or both. Each has 13 natural bars plus two F♯ and one B♭ substitute bars in its range. These are sounding pitches. AG and SG labels distinguish the two parts. Each has its own bright synthesized steel-bar sound, Mixer fader, instrument introduction, manual bar setup, rolls, and independent unused-bar dimming switch in Stage. Existing glockenspiel parts open as Alto glockenspiel with their notes and settings preserved.
  26. Singing melody. In Instruments, enable Singing melody and choose Doo or Gentle flute. Edit melody opens a separate piano roll for the selected section. Click to add; drag to move, or drag the right edge to sustain. Gaps are rests. The fields and note selector also work without dragging. Melody copies and repeats with sections, follows meter and audio alignment, and has its own Mixer volume and mute. The synthesized voice is wordless; lyrics remain on screen. No melody lanes or instruments appear on the board or opening screen.
  27. Unused bars. Stage → Unused bars has a separate switch for each chosen instrument. Turn it on to preserve dark, labeled natural bar spaces inside that instrument’s chosen range; turn it off to show only its selected pitches with no dimming. Choices save with the song and apply in both views. The bass setup and matching independent bass part share one instrument switch. Contrabass keeps only its individual selected bars. These choices are independent of the removable-bar setup screen.
  28. Add lyrics. Open Lyrics below the note editor or use the Lyrics button above the stage. Paste one short phrase per line into the selected section. Choose Sing-Along, Clean, or Storybook, and adjust size and early display. Tap to time all lines plays the selected section: after the count-in, press Space or the tap button as each phrase begins. Taps follow the Lyric timing step, separate from the note grid. Finish timing keeps completed cues; untimed lines stay hidden. Undo restores the previous timing. Fine-tune measure and beat fields, or drag cues along the lyrics timeline (arrow keys also work). In 6/8, subdivisions are 1–6. An empty clear cue hides lyrics for an instrumental passage. Lyrics repeat and copy with their section; copy a section for a different verse. The display reserves space above the lanes and appears during performance.
  29. Rehearse. Hide other tube parts, loop a section, and turn on guide notes. The loop repeats without another count-in. Perform hides the editor.
  30. Save project downloads an editable project with reusable sections, song order, and embedded audio. Older single-pattern projects open as one A Section; older section-based projects keep every section and their original timing. New saves use the mixed-meter format and need this version or later. Nothing is automatically saved. Open it later using Open project.
  31. Keep notes simple. Moving notes have no pitch text by default. In Stage settings, turn on Show pitch names on moving notes if you want them. Bar and target pitches have their own switch. Full instrument names stay below the lanes.
  32. Find your instrument. Barred instruments have full names below their lanes in both play views. Stage → Find your bars shows the complete 13-position instrument layout with the song’s notes glowing, plus first-C and next-C landmarks. Group mode gives each instrument its own page with its highlighted rack. All together uses up to two instruments per bar-preview screen for six seconds, then fades before the count-in. Bar-removal tips can share this screen. Contrabass bars keep their name on the playboard but need no full-rack preview. Preview screen has Previous/Next controls when needed.

Boomwhacker guide notes use the embedded recordings by default, with a synthesized option. Twelve percussion parts use embedded recordings by default, including the three Tubanos; the three hand drums use synthesized practice sounds. Hidden tube parts are silent. Space and R shortcuts work in the workspace. On a focused button, Space activates that button; text fields keep their usual keyboard behavior.

Choose your background in the Stage tab, including Pirate Ship, Backrooms, Farm, Music Classroom, and concert stages. Backdrops and gentle effects appear during performance. Tube colors and the strike line stay consistent across styles. Your projects and imported audio are processed in your browser. Use Save project to keep a copy on your computer, and Open project to continue it later. An internet connection loads the site and its built-in artwork and sounds.
