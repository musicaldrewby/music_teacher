Immersive studio

SR-08WEBXRONE INSTRUMENT · TWO VIEWS

A dedicated synth room for Meta Quest 3. Your sounds, effects, eight tracks and scenes stay together in the same app.

Checking immersive VR support…

Your spatial workstation

  • Mallets: choose Strike or Hold on the VR sound panel. Strike responds to stroke speed; lift between hits. Hold keeps a note on while the mallet touches its bar, then releases when you lift. Hold uses the keyboard velocity setting. Tools → VR room & mallets changes the height, strike settings and 13-bar / chromatic layout. Any selected sound can play; Load xylophone chooses that patch.
  • Grip sustain pedal: enable it in Mallets / Keys. Squeeze either grip while pointing at the instrument or away from panels to sustain notes, even after lifting the mallets. Release both grips to release the pedal. Grabbing a panel takes priority. Piano and percussion sounds still decay naturally according to their SoundFont.
  • Movable panels: aim at a title bar and hold grip or trigger to move it. While holding, use the thumbstick up/down to bring it closer or farther. Press B or Y to recenter and restore all panels.
  • Mixer: drag the raised faders for all eight channels. Volume, pan, reverb and delay sends, Mute and Solo remain live during playback.
  • Pattern & Song: draw, select, move and resize notes, edit drums and controller events, use step recording, and arrange your scene patterns into songs.
  • Tools: recording, quantization, note and measure editing, sound shaping, effects, MIDI, metronome, performances, song files and exports. Type names with the VR keyboard.
  • Save in VR: Tools → Saved songs keeps named copies on this headset. Performances can also be saved and loaded inside VR. Import/export files and device permissions briefly return to the browser; tap Continue, then Enter VR to return.
  • Keyboard option: Tools → VR room & mallets switches back to piano keys. Aim and hold the trigger to play keys or bars.

Quest controllers operate both mallets and panels. Your room layout is remembered on this device. Computer and headset storage are separate; use song files to transfer projects.

LCD controls

Sounds: tap the patch name or a category to browse. Previous and Next turn instrument pages. Search opens a separate page; Show results returns to your choices. Stars save favorites on this device.

Shaping: Tone contains Attack, Release and Brightness. Pitch contains Octave and Fine tune. These adjust the selected part; the SoundFont determines how each sound responds. Tap a numeric value to enter it precisely or reset it.

Effects: choose Reverb or Delay, then Main or Tone. Reverb Main sets amount, space and decay; Tone sets damping and pre-delay. Delay Main sets amount, stereo width and time; Tone sets feedback and damping. Both effects are shared; Mixer controls each track’s send.

Presets: Recall selects and loads an eight-part setup. Save names or updates a setup. Files imports, exports or deletes the selected preset. Loading keeps your scene notes and song order.

Display: Compact shortens the LCD and shows fewer sounds per page. Its size stays fixed as you navigate. Short screens use the main workspace scroll. MIDI and metronome options are in Settings.

A room for your sounds.

Finding controls: PLAY chooses sounds and triggers scenes; PATTERN records and edits a scene; SONG arranges scenes; MIXER balances the shared eight-part performance. Project contains files and Export. Settings contains MIDI and metronome options. Record settings contains count-in, Overdub/Record over, recording grid, meter and punch. The LCD has Sounds, Sound shaping, Effects and Performances. The Inspector combines Track, Selected notes and Pattern actions in one side panel. Selecting notes updates its fields without opening it. Panels close with Close or Escape; closing a panel leaves playback running.

Finding notes: Opening PATTERN or selecting a different track or scene centers the piano roll on that part’s notes. Empty parts start around middle C; drum parts start around common drum pitches. Find notes recenters the view anytime. Editing and zoom changes preserve your manual vertical scroll position.

Step recording: In PATTERN, choose Piano roll or Drum steps, then turn on Step recording and choose a note length. Play notes or chords from MIDI, touch or your computer keyboard; releasing the final key advances the cursor. Rest skips a step, Tie extends the last chord, and Back moves without erasing. Show keyboard reveals the on-screen keys. Stop, changing track/scene or leaving PATTERN ends step entry.

Performance presets: The LCD’s Performances page saves your eight sounds, shaping, mixer and effects for reuse. Load selected leaves all scene patterns and song order intact. Export/Import moves presets between devices; local presets do not sync automatically.

Play and Song: PLAY’s compact track strip selects, arms (R) and mutes (M) parts. SONG shows proportional scene blocks with repeats and a moving playhead. Use a dotted drag handle or Earlier/Later to change order, and Duplicate to reuse a scene. The selected-entry menu also reaches small blocks in long songs. Change an entry’s scene or repeats there. Play song is in the persistent transport below.

Performance, scenes and song. One shared performance holds eight instruments with their names, shaping, volume, pan, mute/solo and effect sends. Exactly eight scene buttons select independent patterns, each with a custom name and 1–256 measures (four by default). Changing an instrument or mixer setting changes it across all eight scenes. PATTERN records and edits the selected scene. SONG arranges references to those scenes with repeats; editing a pattern updates all its song entries. Copy a pattern to another scene to make a variation.

Changing scenes. Use number keys 1–8 (including the numeric keypad) or the scene buttons. While stopped, selection opens that pattern immediately. During pattern playback, the chosen scene flashes and starts on beat 1 of the next measure. A press on beat 1 waits for the following measure. Choosing another scene before the boundary replaces the queued choice. Number shortcuts are inactive while typing in a field, using a modifier key, or viewing Help. The audio thread changes patterns without replacing instruments or clearing reverb/delay tails. Finish recording before changing scenes. During Song playback the scene lights follow the arrangement; stop before selecting a pattern to edit.

Recording and editing. Arm one track with R, then record notes, velocity, sustain, modulation, expression, pitch bend and pressure from a MIDI controller. Computer keys and the touch keyboard can record too. Overdub adds notes; Record over replaces the selected scene’s armed track, or only its punch range when Punch is enabled. Drum steps and expression lanes edit the current pattern. Measure operations can affect one part or all eight within this scene.

Exports. Choose the selected pattern or complete song order. MIDI includes tempo, meter, scene markers, patches and controller data. WAV uses your SoundFont and shared mix/effects in a background worker. Audio is 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo; separate tracks are aligned in a ZIP. Lower master volume and re-export if clipping is reported. Large audio exports have a memory limit; MIDI remains available.

Metronome. Open Metronome below the transport to choose Bright click, Wood block, Rim tap or Digital beep. Preview one bar auditions the current tempo and meter without playing or recording your pattern. The click volume and beat-one accent can be adjusted while the sequencer runs. Count-in clicks even when the metronome is Off; set its volume to 0% for silence. Metronome settings save with the song. Exports exclude the click.

Tempo. TEMPO / BPM stays in the bottom transport beside Play and Record, in every workspace. Stop playback, choose a scene, then type its BPM or use − / +. Each of the eight patterns saves its own tempo. Queued scenes adopt their tempo on beat 1 of the next measure; Song mode follows each pattern’s tempo automatically. The BPM display follows the playing pattern, and MIDI, WAV and track exports keep all tempo changes. Older songs retain their original tempo in every scene.

Timing. Grid resolution only changes the lines. Snap affects manual edits. Record quantization independently controls recorded note starts and defaults to sixteenth notes. Choose Off to keep free timing. Notes played up to 150 ms before the first downbeat are captured at beat one, even with quantization Off; earlier count-in notes are ignored. Post-record quantization offers strength, swing and optional note-length correction, with Restore original timing. Timing and note lengths use quarter-note beats; a 6/8 bar contains three quarter-note beats. Notes held over the loop end retain their full duration. Playback wraps them across the boundary when Loop is on; quantization moves each played note as a whole. Stop finishes held notes. Changing tempo preserves recorded beat positions.

Projects. Save song stays beside the song name. The Project menu contains Open song, New song, Restore previous session, and an Export music shortcut to the Song export panel. Save song downloads the song name, shared eight-part performance, all eight scene patterns, scene names and lengths, song order, mix, shaping and effects together as a JSON file. Open song restores it using the included GeneralUser bank. Autosave restores your last session in this browser on this device. Previous session restores the backup kept when starting a new project or opening another one. Save song provides a portable backup. Older project files import into Scene 1, with an arranged song in Scene 2 when available. The complete original project remains downloadable in SONG. Browser storage can be cleared; keep downloaded project files too. Undo/Redo covers note edits, recording takes and project replacement. Mixer adjustments are stored when you save. Playback stops when the page loses focus.

Choose Green LCD for an XP-80-inspired display, or Purple LCD for a K5000-inspired purple and lavender display. Both use original dense 5×7 pixel lettering, including lowercase patch names. The green screen follows your yellow-green LCD reference. Your choice is remembered in this browser. Changing the display does not change the sound.

This site includes GeneralUser GS 1.471 with 269 patches. Enable sound to load the included samples. Green LCD and Purple LCD are beside Mixer in every workspace.

Click Enable sound once. Tap the instrument name or ALL / GM / VARIATIONS / DRUMS inside the large LCD to browse sounds. Previous and Next turn pages; Search opens name and family filters. Star sounds to keep them in Favorites. Recent lists your last 24 sound choices. Both are remembered on this device, including in VR. The highlighted sound loads into the part named at the top and stays shared across all eight scenes. Play your keyboard or a demo to audition it; the browser stays open until Done returns to the main display. The same demo follows your selected patch. Drum patches reveal percussion pads and drum demos. Program numbers are shown as 1–128; bank numbers are shown as stored in the SoundFont. C4 is MIDI note 60.

Type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P ; to play. Hold Space for sustain, use Z/X to shift octaves, and Escape to stop. Choose 25, 37, or 49 visible keys. Use the octave buttons to reach other notes. Mouse drags and multiple touches can play notes. Click outside a search field or slider to resume typing notes.

Play layout. All four workspaces share the same width and toolbar positions. Compact layout shortens the LCD and tightens panel spacing while keeping touch controls available; scroll inside the LCD to reach its full menus. This choice is remembered on this device. The LCD comes first in Play, with Sounds, Sound shaping, Reverb, Delay and MIDI input pages. The keyboard is directly below it, followed by scenes and recording controls. Pattern and Song remain separate workspaces. Mixer keeps master volume and shortcuts back to the LCD effects.
One patch per track. Choose a track, choose its patch, and play. Sound shaping adjusts octave, fine tuning, attack, release and brightness for that track. Octave transposition is captured into recorded notes; tuning and timbre also affect playback. Notes transposed outside the MIDI range are silent.

Exact values. Tap a displayed slider value to type a number or reset it. Mixer pan uses 0 for center, negative for left and positive for right; level and send values use percent. Delay time chooses the nearest supported time in milliseconds. The transport shows the selected part separately from the recording destination. Scene labels show Playing and Next measure; their progress bars follow the current measure.

Effects. Reverb offers room, hall and plate spaces, with amount, decay, damping and pre-delay. Delay has amount, time, feedback, damping, and stereo or centered echoes. Turn either effect off to bypass its wet output. Decay is shown on the engine’s 0–127 scale; delay and pre-delay are in milliseconds. Reset effects restores a modest reverb and switches delay off.

MIDI. Connect your controller, enable sound, then choose Connect MIDI and grant browser access. Pick one input or all inputs and an optional receive channel. Incoming bank and program changes do not replace your selected sounds. If Web MIDI is unavailable, the panel explains that the touch keyboard is still available. Desktop Chrome and Edge support Web MIDI; iPad browser support varies. MIDI hardware needs a browser with Web MIDI support.

iPad and touch. Multiple fingers and gliding between keys are supported. Expand keyboard makes more room for playing. PATTERN opens the sequencer and MIXER shows the shared performance controls. Full screen is offered when supported by the browser. You can also add the site to your Home Screen on iPad. The keyboard and controls suppress text selection and play-area gestures while the rest of the page can scroll. Sound stops when the app loses focus.
